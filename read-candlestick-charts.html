How to Read a Candlestick Chart: A Beginner Guide for TradingView

Updated October 2026 · Money Moves India

A candlestick chart shows what price did in a set period, such as one day. Each candle holds four prices. Once you know them, any chart on TradingView or a broker app becomes readable.

The parts of one candle

PartMeaning
OpenPrice at the start of the period
ClosePrice at the end of the period
HighHighest price reached (top of the thin line)
LowLowest price reached (bottom of the thin line)
BodyThe thick part between open and close
WickThe thin lines above and below the body

On most free charts a green candle means the close was higher than the open, and a red candle means the close was lower. Colours can be changed in settings, so check yours.

Worked example

A stock opens at Rs 100, rises to a high of Rs 108, falls to a low of Rs 97 and closes at Rs 105. This is a green candle. The body runs from 100 to 105, which is Rs 5. The upper wick is Rs 3 (105 to 108) and the lower wick is Rs 3 (97 to 100).

Timeframes change the picture

A daily candle sums up one day. A 5-minute candle sums up five minutes. The same stock can look like it is rising on the daily chart and falling on the 5-minute chart. Start with the daily chart because it has less noise.

Three practice exercises

  1. Open TradingView, pick any stock and set the daily timeframe. Find the last green and last red candle and write down open and close for each.
  2. Find the candle with the longest upper wick in the last month. What does a long wick tell you about the day?
  3. Switch to the 1-hour timeframe and describe how the same week looks different.

Education only. Reading a chart does not predict the future, and trading involves risk.
